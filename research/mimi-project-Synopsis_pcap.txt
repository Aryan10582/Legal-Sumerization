1.
 
Title
 
of
 
the
 
Problem
 AI-Based  Summarization  and  Clause  Classification  of  Indian  Legal  Documents  Using  NLP  
and
 
Transformer
 
Models.
 
2.
 
Introduction
 India's  legal  system  faces  a  severe  backlog,  with  millions  of  pending  cases,  heavy  worded  
judgments
 
and
 
contracts
 
that
 
are
 
time-consuming
 
for
  
professionals
 
to
 
review
 
manually.
 
Natural
 
Language
 
Processing
 
(NLP)
 
and
 
transformer-based
 
language
 
models
 
such
 
as
 
BERT
 
have
 
shown
 
strong
 
potential
 
for
 
automating
 
legal
 
text
 
analysis,However,
 
Indian
 
legal
 
documents
 
pose
 
unique
 
challenges:
 
they
 
are
 
often
 
unstructured,
 
and
 
stylistically
 
distinct
 
from
 
Western
 
case
 
law.
 
Prior
 
work
 
on
 
domain-specific
 
summarization
 
models
 
and
 
legal
 
transformer
 
variants
 
has
 
demonstrated
 
improved
 
performance
 
over
 
generic
 
NLP
 
when
 
adapted
 
to
 
Indian
 
legal
 
text
 
.
 
This
 
project
 
proposes
 
an
 
NLP-based
 
system
 
that
 
automatically
 
summarizes
 
Indian
 
court
 
judgments
 
and
 
classifies
 
clauses
 
into
 
legal
 
categories,
 
using
 
transformer
 
fine-tuned
 
on
 
Indian
 
legal
 
code.
 
By
 
reducing
 
the
 
manual
 
effort
 
required
 
to
 
review
 
lengthy
 
legal
 
documents,
 
the
 
system
 
aims
 
to
 
support
 
lawyers,
 
legal
 
researchers,
 
and
 
self-represented
 
litigants
 
in
 
navigating
 
India's
 
legal
 
system
 
more
 
efficiently.
 
3.
 
Keywords
 Legal  NLP;  Document  Summarization;  Clause  Classification;  Transformer  Models;  
LegalBERT;
 
Indian
 
Judiciary.
 
4.
 
Objectives
 
•
 
To  collect  and  pre-process  Indian  legal  documents  while  handling  noisy  OCR  text,  
inconsistent
 
formatting,
 
and
 
document-length
 
variation.
 
•
 
To  design  and  fine-tune  transformer-based  models  for  automatic  summarization  of  
judgments
 
and
 
classification
 
of
 
clauses.
 
•
 
To  evaluate  model  performance  using  standard  metrics  —  ROUGE  for  summarization  
and
 
F1-score/accuracy
 
for
 
classification.
 
5.
 
Methodology
 
•
 
Data  Acquisition:  Collect  Indian  court  judgments  from  public  repositories  such  as  
IndianKanoon
 
and
 
the
 
e-Courts
 
National
 
Judicial
 
Data
 
Grid,
 
and
 
contract
 
clauses
 
from
 
open
 
datasets
 
such
 
as
 
CUAD,
 
for
 
classification
 
pretraining.
 
•
 
Data  Pre-processing:  Clean  and  normalise  legal  text  and  construct  labelled  datasets  for  
clause
 
categories.

•
 
Feature  Engineering:  Generate  contextual  embeddings  using  pre-trained  legal  
transformer
 
models
 
(LegalBERT/InLegalBERT)
 
as
 
input
 
representations.
 
•
 
Model  Development:  Fine-tune  transformer-based  summarization  models  
(Legal-Pegasus,
 
BART,
 
Longformer)
 
for
 
judgment
 
summarization,
 
and
 
classifiers
 
for
 
contract
 
clause
 
classification,
 
using
 
Python,
 
Hugging
 
Face
 
Transformers,
 
and
 
PyTorch.
 
•
 
Model  Evaluation:  Compare  fine-tuned  transformer  models  against  classical  ML  
baselines
 
(TF-IDF
 
+
 
SVM/Logistic
 
Regression)
 
using
 
ROUGE,
 
F1-score,
 
and
 
accuracy
 
on
 
held-out
 
Indian
 
legal
 
test
 
data.
 
•
 
Analysis  and  Reporting:  Analyse  results,  identify  the  best-performing  configuration,  and  
document
 
findings
 
in
 
the
 
final
 
project
 
report.

Data
 
Source
 Primary  data  will  be  sourced  from  publicly  available  Indian  legal  judgment  repositories  such  
as
 
IndianKanoon
 
and
 
the
 
e-Courts
 
National
 
Judicial
 
Data,
 
which
 
provide
 
free-text

Indian
 
court

For

project
 
will
 
additionally
 
use
 
open
 
benchmark
 
datasets
 
such
 
as

Contract
 
Understanding
 
Atticus

(CUAD)

pretraining

cross-domain
 
evaluation,
 
supplemented
 
by
 
publicly
 
available
 
Indian
 
legal-document

referenced
 
in
 
prior
 
work
 
.
 
7.
 
Plan
 
of
 
Execution
 Phase  Activity  Phase  1  finalisation  of  datasets,  clause  taxonomy,  and  models  Phase  2  Data  acquisition  (IndianKanoon,  e-Courts,  CUAD)  and  data  pre-processing  Phase  3  data  analysis  and  dataset  labelling  Phase  4  Implementation  of  baseline  models  (TF-IDF  +  SVM  /  Logistic  Regression)  Phase  5  Fine-tuning  of  transformer  models  (LegalBERT,  Legal-Pegasus/BART)  Phase  6  Comparative  evaluation,  hyper-parameter  tuning  Phase  7  Report  writing,  documentation,  and  final  presentation
